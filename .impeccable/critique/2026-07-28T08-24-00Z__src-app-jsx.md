## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This reads as authored for its actual context — one-handed, hands messy, standing at a stove — not a generic recipe CRUD app. Three concrete tells: `CookMode`'s wake-lock + swipe-tuned + `endsAt`-driven timers solve the real failure mode of kitchen timer apps (drift while backgrounded); the pan/serving/batch scaler is ingredient-aware rather than a naive multiplier, down to warning that bake time shifts with pan depth; and the copy/theming is unapologetically Australian (Olive Grove, Musk Stick, fan-forced ovens, AU cup conversions) rather than corporate-neutral. This verdict isn't uniform across the app, though — the list view and the empty planner read as if they were extended by volume rather than redesigned for volume, which is where the priority issues below concentrate.

**Deterministic scan:** The detector flagged 2 findings via static scan (`overused-font` at 86–93% of text on `body`; a `width`-based CSS `transition` at line 5525), and 22 findings via live browser scan across 5 views — dominated by **15 low-contrast instances**, split between one recurring pattern (secondary/muted text at **3.3:1**, needs 4.5:1, appearing 14× on the Recipes list alone) and a near-miss button at 4.2:1 on the recipe detail page. This is a real gap between the two assessments: Assessment A's persona selection (Casey, Riley, Jordan) didn't include Sam (accessibility-dependent, screen reader/keyboard-only), so the systemic contrast failure went unscored in the heuristics table above — it's the clearest case here of the detector catching what the design review missed. The `overused-font` finding is likely a **false positive**: the app deliberately uses one body typeface (Instrument Sans) consistently by design, which is normally a *strength*, not slop — not treated as a real issue. The recurring 35-char `all-caps-body` span found identically on Plan/Shop/Tips tabs is almost certainly a shared chrome element (a section-header label style), worth a quick look but not urgent.

**Visual overlays:** Browser injection succeeded on a second attempt after a first pass was lost to an app reload; the live-server session was stopped per cleanup requirements, so there's no `[Human]` tab overlay currently visible — the findings above are the full record of what the overlay would have shown.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-crafted, specifically-authored app that gets the hard, unglamorous parts right (timer drift, undo safety, screen-wake-lock) and falls down in exactly the place volume growth usually breaks a design: the list view. The biggest opportunity is narrow and concrete — the Recipes tab (now ~200 titles) still defaults to "show everything, alphabetically, filters collapsed," which fights the app's own "one-handed, impatient, mid-cook" design thesis everywhere else.

## What's Working

1. **CookMode's screen-wake-lock + `endsAt`-driven timers** — this is software written by someone who's actually hit the "phone went to sleep and my timer stopped" bug mid-cook, and fixed the real cause instead of papering over it.
2. **The shop-picker mediation layer** — one reusable confirmation surface for "send to shopping list" (single recipe, week, or bake plan) that defaults pantry staples unchecked instead of silently merging. Same component, three call sites, no bespoke duplication.
3. **Reverse scaling ("I have 300g of flour")** — a genuinely uncommon feature, well-targeted at the actual pain point of "the recipe wants more than I have."

## Priority Issues

**[P1] "Reset everything" is one tap away from wiping the entire local recipe collection**
Why it matters: this app has no backend by default — local storage *is* the only copy of a hand-curated collection, and the button sits in the same styled row, same tap weight, as "Restore starter recipes." A 10-second undo toast that can be missed (phone set down, tab backgrounded) is a thin safety net for the single highest-consequence action in the app.
Fix: give this one action a heavier gate than every other destructive action — even a 2-tap "tap again to confirm" state change would signal it's categorically different.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Systemic low-contrast text — 3.3:1 where WCAG AA requires 4.5:1, on 14+ elements per Recipes-list render**
Why it matters: this is a real accessibility failure the design review's chosen personas didn't surface (Sam wasn't selected), caught only by the mechanical scan. It affects what's almost certainly the secondary/muted text token pair (`#6e7663` on `#1f251b`) app-wide, plus a near-miss button (4.2:1) on the recipe detail page.
Fix: audit the muted-text token pair for dark surfaces and lift it to at least 4.5:1; the button color is close enough that a small lightness bump likely clears it.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] The Recipes list doesn't scale to the app's own recipe count**
Why it matters: ~200 cards render unpaginated and unvirtualized in one scroll, filters are hidden behind a collapsed disclosure by default, and sort defaults to full A–Z — directly working against the "one-handed, impatient, mid-cook" thesis the rest of the app nails.
Fix: default the list to something curated (Favourites or Recently Cooked) instead of full A–Z; consider a search-first pattern; virtualize the grid regardless of what else changes.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Duplicate and near-duplicate seed recipes create real ambiguity, not just data mess**
Why it matters: at least a dozen exact-title collisions exist in the seed data (two "Gingerbread Biscuits," two "Chocolate Weet-Bix Slice," two "Earl Grey Cake," etc.) plus intentional 23cm/24cm near-duplicates. The review caught "You might also like" recommending the same-titled recipe as if it were a different one — that reads as a bug to a real user, and there's no UI support for "which one did I actually cook."
Fix: de-duplicate, or disambiguate titles for intentional variants ("Gingerbread Biscuits (soft)" vs "(crisp)").
Suggested command: `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] Recipe-paste import silently drops lines that fail to parse**
Why it matters: `parseIngredientLine` and step parsing filter out unparseable lines with only a generic success count reported — a user has no way to know a line was dropped versus successfully parsed with no amount.
Fix: surface which raw lines didn't parse, even as a small "2 lines couldn't be understood" note with the raw text shown.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted, thumb-only, one-handed mid-cook)**: the Filters disclosure defaults closed, adding a tap before category/skill/diet chips are reachable; "Reset everything" sits directly adjacent to "Restore starter recipes" in the same styled row — exactly the adjacent-target mis-tap that happens to a distracted thumb. CookMode's large touch targets and swipe gestures are the counter-example done right for this persona.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent, not originally selected but demanded by the evidence)**: the detector's 14+ instances of 3.3:1 text contrast on the Recipes list fail WCAG AA outright for anyone with low vision. Diet tags ("Vegetarian," "Gluten-free") are heuristically inferred from ingredient keywords with a caveat that's disclosed but buried under a collapsed filter drawer — for a user relying on that tag for a real allergy, this is a safety-relevant discoverability gap, not just a UX nicety.

**Riley (deliberate stress tester)**: typing servings past 40 or minutes past 600 silently clamps with zero explanation — Riley reads a non-responding input as broken. The duplicate-recipe seed data is exactly the kind of "does this app know its own state" issue this persona flags on first open.

## Minor Observations

- `layout-transition` detector finding at `App.jsx:5525` (`transition: width`) — likely animates a progress or scaler bar; width transitions are layout-triggering and janky under load compared to a `transform: scaleX` equivalent.
- The recurring 35-char `all-caps-body` span across Plan/Shop/Tips tabs is probably one shared header-label component — confirm it's not a stray uppercase-transform on user-entered text.
- Empty planner week shows 14 independent "Nothing planned" strings (7 days × 2 slots) — minor scan fatigue, low urgency.
- The favourite-star scrim over photo cards (`rgba(0,0,0,0.35)`) has a code comment citing a measured 2.98:1 contrast fix — good evidence the team already fights this class of bug elsewhere; the systemic muted-text contrast issue above is the one that got past that habit.

## Questions to Consider

- If the target user genuinely has ~200 recipes and one free hand, is a searchable/filterable *list* the right default view at all — or should the landing state be "this week's plan" / "cook this again," with the full catalog demoted to an explicit "browse everything" mode?
- The app clearly invested real design energy in reversibility (undo toasts everywhere) as its answer to destructive-action anxiety — but "reset everything" gets the same lightweight pattern as deleting one shopping item. Is a severity-scaled confirmation worth the one-off inconsistency, or is a periodic automatic local backup a cheaper way to make the existing pattern trustworthy even for the worst case?
- Diet tags are explicitly caveated as unreliable for allergies. Given this is a family kitchen app where real dietary restrictions plausibly matter, should that caveat be load-bearing UI rather than a footnote under a collapsed drawer?
